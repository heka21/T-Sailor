The Swan River looks wide and open, but much of it is **shallow**. A keelboat’s keel may draw 1.5–2 metres, so
knowing where the deep water is matters.

## Lateral marks

Channels are marked with red and green marks. Australia uses **IALA Region A**:

<Callout type="remember" title="Which way is ‘in’?">
  The direction of buoyage is generally <strong>from the sea, upstream</strong>. Heading up the river from Fremantle,
  red marks are on your left (port). On the way back down to the sea, keep reds on your right.
</Callout>

## Other marks you may see

| Mark | Looks like | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cardinal** | Yellow and black, two cone topmark | Safe water is on the named side (N, E, S, W) |
| **Isolated danger** | Black with red band, two black balls | Danger right here — keep clear all round |
| **Safe water** | Red and white vertical stripes, red ball | Deep water all around (e.g. channel entrance) |
| **Special mark** | Yellow, X topmark | Special area — e.g. a speed zone, swimming area, racing mark |

## Hazards on the Swan

- **Shallows and sandbars** — large parts of the river are less than 2 m deep, especially near points and spits.
  Learn your boat’s **draft**, watch the depth sounder, and stay in marked or well-known deep water.
- **Ferries and large vessels** in the channels — they can’t easily turn or stop. Keep well clear.
- **Bridges** — check the clearance under the Narrows and Causeway before ever sailing upstream; a keelboat mast won’t fit.
- **Speed limits** — much of the river has 8-knot limits and some areas are restricted. Look for signs and yellow special marks.
- **Swimmers, kayaks, rowers and kitesurfers** — especially near beaches on summer afternoons.

<Callout type="note" title="Local knowledge">
  This lesson covers general principles. Ask your instructors about the specific shallows, marks and no-go areas
  around Matilda Bay and the club’s usual sailing area, and always carry a chart or chart app.
</Callout>

## A simple passage plan

1. **Where** — route, waypoints, hazards along the way.
2. **When** — departure, return, and when the wind will change.
3. **Who** — crew and their experience.
4. **What if** — bail-out options and safe havens if the breeze is too much.
5. **Tell someone** — log your departure with the club or a friend ashore.
