A **spinnaker** (“kite”) is a big, light, balloon-shaped sail for sailing downwind. It can nearly double the sail
area — and the fun. It also has more lines than anything else on the boat, so learning the gear comes first.

## Symmetric vs asymmetric

- **Symmetric spinnaker** — both edges are the same. Flown from a **pole** on the windward side. Great for running deep downwind. This is the type this course focuses on.
- **Asymmetric (gennaker)** — shaped like a big jib, its tack is fixed at the bow or on a bowsprit. Easier to handle, best on a broad reach.

<Callout type="remember" title="Guy and sheet swap at every gybe">
  The guy and sheet are just names for <em>where</em> a line is. The windward one is the guy; the leeward one is the
  sheet. After a gybe, the old sheet becomes the new guy.
</Callout>

## Packing the kite

A clean hoist starts with a clean pack. Run down both edges (the **luffs**) from the head so they’re not twisted, then
stuff the middle into the bag with the **three corners on top** — head in the middle, tack and clew either side.
Clip the bag on the leeward side, ready for the hoist.

## Rigging check

- Halyard, sheet and guy led **outside everything** (forestay, lifelines, jib sheets) — trace each one from the corner back to the cockpit.
- Stopper knots in the ends of the sheet and guy.
- Pole stowed and ready, topping lift and downhaul attached.
