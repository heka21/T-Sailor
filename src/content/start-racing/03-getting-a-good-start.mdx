A good start means: **on the line, at full speed, in clear air, at the right end, when the gun goes.** That’s a
lot to get right at once — so break it down.

## 1. Which end is favoured?

If the line isn’t square to the wind, one end is closer to the windward mark. Drag the ends and shift the wind:

<StartLine />

**On the water:** sail along the line, or go head to wind in the middle — your bow points towards the favoured end.

## 2. Where is the line?

From the middle of the line it’s very hard to judge where the line is. Use a **transit**: before the start, sit on
the extension of the line beyond the pin and line up the committee boat’s flag with something on shore. During the
start, when the committee boat and that landmark line up, you’re on the line.

## 3. Time on distance

Know how long your boat takes to accelerate and how far it travels in, say, 30 seconds. Then you can hold back and
**time your run** so you hit the line at full speed just as the gun goes.

## 4. Clear air

Behind and to leeward of other boats the wind is disturbed (“dirty air”). Leave a **gap to leeward** — a hole you can
sail into to accelerate and point high.

## 5. A simple plan for your first starts

1. 2 minutes: be near the favoured end, a few boat lengths behind the line.
2. 1 minute: find your gap. Sails eased, boat slow but steering.
3. 20–30 seconds: protect your gap (luff boats that try to take it, within the rules).
4. ~10 seconds: sheet in and accelerate.
5. Gun: full speed, close-hauled, look for clear air.

<Callout type="safety" title="Starboard!">
  The start is the most crowded part of a race. Most boats approach on starboard tack — avoid sailing into the pack on
  port. Keep a lookout and call clearly.
</Callout>
