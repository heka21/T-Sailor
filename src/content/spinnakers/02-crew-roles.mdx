A spinnaker set is a team play. Everyone needs to know their job and the call that triggers it.

<CrewPositions spinnaker />

## Calls you’ll hear

| Call | Who | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Pole up!” | Bow | Pole is clipped on and raised to height |
| “Hoist!” | Helm/tactician | Pit hauls the halyard as fast as possible |
| “Made!” | Pit | Halyard is all the way up and cleated |
| “Gybing!” | Helm | Starting the gybe |
| “Made!” / “Pole on!” | Bow | Pole is attached on the new side |
| “Drop!” | Helm | Release the halyard and gather the sail |

## The trimmer’s job

The spinnaker trimmer never stops: easing the sheet until the luff starts to **curl**, then trimming just enough to
stop it collapsing. They also call the pressure to the helm: “Pressure good / Need to come up / Can go lower”.

## Helm and trimmer together

When the kite is about to collapse, the helm can **bear away** to fill it as the trimmer trims. In a gust the helm
**bears away** to stay under control and the trimmer eases. Talking constantly keeps the boat fast and upright.

<Callout type="tip">
  On small keelboats one person may do several of these jobs. Learn them all — rotating positions is the fastest way
  to understand the whole manoeuvre.
</Callout>
