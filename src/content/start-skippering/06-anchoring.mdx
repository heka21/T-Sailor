Anchoring lets you stop for lunch or a swim, wait out a problem, or hold position if the engine fails. It’s also an
emergency tool: if you’re drifting towards danger, **drop the anchor**.

## Choose your spot

- **Depth** — enough water for your keel at low tide, but not so deep you lack rode.
- **Bottom** — sand and mud hold well; weed and rock don’t.
- **Shelter** — out of the wind and waves, with room to swing in a full circle without hitting anything.
- **Not in a channel**, and clear of moorings and cables.

## How much rode?

The **scope** is the length of anchor line (rode) compared with the depth. Too short and the anchor gets pulled up
and out of the seabed.

## Step by step

1. **Brief the crew** and prepare: anchor ready, rode flaked out so it runs free, end of the rode tied to the boat!
2. **Approach slowly into the wind** (or current) to your chosen spot.
3. **Stop**, and lower — don’t throw — the anchor to the bottom as the boat begins to drift back.
4. **Pay out** rode as the boat drifts back, until you have the right scope.
5. **Set it** — snub the rode on the cleat; under engine, go gently astern to dig the anchor in.
6. **Check it holds** — line up two objects on shore (a **transit**). If they move apart, you’re dragging.

## Weighing (lifting) anchor

Motor or sail gently up towards the anchor while the crew pulls in the rode. Once directly above, a firm pull breaks
it out. Keep the deck clear of mud and chain.
