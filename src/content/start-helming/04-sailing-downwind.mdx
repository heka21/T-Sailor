Downwind sailing feels relaxed — the boat is upright and the apparent wind is light. But it hides the most common
danger on a keelboat: the **accidental gybe**.

## Steering downwind

Upwind you steer to the telltales. Downwind:

- Feel the wind on the **back of your neck and ears**.
- Watch the **windex** — keep its arrow pointing a little to the opposite side from the boom.
- Watch the **jib**. When you get close to dead downwind it collapses behind the main — a warning you’re near the gybe zone.
- Keep the boom **out**, but not so far that the mainsail presses against the shrouds.

## By the lee

If you turn past dead downwind, the wind starts to come over the **same side as the boom**. That’s sailing **by the lee**,
and the boom may slam across without warning.

## Rolling and broaching

Downwind in a breeze the boat may roll from side to side. Keep the helm movements small and steady, and keep the
boom vang tight so the boom doesn’t lift. If the boat suddenly rounds up into the wind (a **broach**), ease the main
quickly and steer down again.

## Downwind is VMG

Sailing a little hotter (towards a broad reach) is often faster to a downwind mark than sailing dead downwind, even
though the distance is longer — and much safer. Racers gybe downwind just like they tack upwind.
