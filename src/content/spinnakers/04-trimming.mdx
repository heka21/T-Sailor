Spinnaker trim is all about **two lines**: the **guy** (pole angle) and the **sheet** (how far the sail is eased).

<Spinnaker />

## Pole angle — the guy

Keep the pole roughly **at right angles to the apparent wind**:

- **Reaching** — pole forward, nearly on the forestay.
- **Running** — pole squared right back, nearly at right angles to the boat.

A quick check: look at the windex — the pole should point roughly 90° away from it.

## Pole height

- Set the outboard end so the **two clews are level**.
- Pole too high → the luff curls high up first. Lower it.
- Pole too low → the luff curls low down first. Raise it.
- Keep the pole roughly **horizontal** (raise the inboard end on the mast as you raise the outboard end).

## The sheet — play the curl

**Ease until the luff starts to curl, then trim a little.** Repeat continuously. A spinnaker that’s strapped in looks
full but is slow and heels the boat; a spinnaker on the edge of curling is fast.

<Callout type="remember">
  The apparent wind matters here too. As the boat accelerates, the apparent wind moves forward — bring the pole forward
  and trim the sheet.
</Callout>

## Steering with the kite

- Kite collapsing → **bear away** (and trim).
- Too much pressure, boat heeling → **bear away** in the gust, ease the sheet.
- Lots of slack, kite floating → you can **head up** a little for speed.
