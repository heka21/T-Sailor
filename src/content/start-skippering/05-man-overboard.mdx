Someone going overboard is the emergency every skipper trains for. A person in the water is very hard to see and
can get cold quickly — speed and calm teamwork save lives.

## The first seconds

1. **Shout “Man overboard!”** loudly.
2. **Throw** a lifebuoy or anything that floats towards them.
3. **Point** — one person points at the casualty and **never stops pointing**.
4. **Note the position** — press MOB on the GPS/chartplotter if you have one.

## Sailing back: reach – tack – reach

<ManOverboard />

<Callout type="tip" title="Why not just turn around?">
  Turning straight back usually means a gybe with an unprepared crew — dangerous — or arriving too fast and downwind of
  the person. The reach–tack–reach gives time to organise the crew and a controlled, slow final approach.
</Callout>

## Other methods

- **Quick-stop** — tack immediately (leaving the jib backed) and circle back close to the casualty. Keeps the boat near them.
- **Under engine** — drop or furl sails, **check there are no ropes in the water** so the propeller can’t foul, then motor back, stopping with the person well away from the propeller.

Your instructor will show the method your club prefers on the boats you sail.

## Getting them back on board

This is often the hardest part — a wet person in clothes is very heavy.

- Get a **line** on them first so they can’t drift away.
- Use the **swim ladder**, or a **halyard on a winch** attached to their lifejacket’s lifting point.
- Treat for **cold and shock**, and call for help if needed: **VHF channel 16** or **000**.

## Prevention

- Lifejackets on (and done up).
- “One hand for you, one for the boat.”
- Tethers and jacklines in rough conditions or at night.
- Never pee over the side of a moving boat!
