Sailing upwind (close-hauled) is the most demanding job for a helm. The sails are pulled in tight and stay put —
**you** keep them working by steering.

## The groove

There’s a narrow band of angles where the boat is fast and climbing upwind. Too close to the wind and you slow down;
too far and you sail fast but in the wrong direction.

## Steering with the telltales

When sailing upwind, the trimmers set the sails and **the helm steers to the jib telltales**:

- **Windward telltale lifts** → you’re pointing too high (pinching). **Bear away** a touch.
- **Leeward telltale lifts** → you’re too low (footing / stalled). **Head up** a touch.
- With a tiller: move the tiller **towards the telltale that’s lifting**.

## Feel the boat

Telltales aren’t the only clue:

- **Heel** — a sudden increase means a gust. Ease the main or head up slightly to keep the boat on its feet.
- **Sound and speed** — a quiet, slowing boat is often pinching.
- **The tiller** — too much weather helm means too much heel or a main that’s too tight.

<Callout type="tip" title="In waves">
  Steer a little lower (more power) in chop, and use smooth turns over the bigger waves. Flat water lets you point higher.
</Callout>

## Gusts and lulls

| What happens | Helm | Main trimmer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gust hits | Head up slightly (“feather”) | Ease the main to stop excess heel |
| Lull | Bear away slightly to keep speed | Trim back in |
