## The drop

The drop is usually done just before the leeward mark, and it must be finished before you round up.

<Spinnaker mode="drop" />

### Leeward drop

1. **Jib up** first so you can sail upwind straight away.
2. Bear away so the kite is **blanketed by the main**.
3. **Ease the guy** so the pole goes to the forestay and the kite flags behind the main.
4. Crew grab the sheet/foot **under the boom** and gather the sail into the hatch or cockpit.
5. Pit **releases the halyard** at the speed the crew can gather. Never let it drop into the water.
6. Pole down, tidy up, and trim for the beat.

<Callout type="tip" title="Early is better than fast">
  The most common mistake is dropping too late. If you’re racing, start your drop 3–4 boat lengths before the zone.
</Callout>

## The broach

A **broach** is when the boat suddenly rounds up into the wind, heels hard and won’t steer — the rudder loses grip.
It usually happens in a gust with too much spinnaker sheet on.

**Recovering:**

1. **Ease the spinnaker sheet** hard — let it flag.
2. **Ease the vang and the mainsheet** to depower the main.
3. Once the boat comes back upright and the rudder bites, **bear away** and re-trim.

**Preventing:**

- Bear away in the gusts; keep the boat flat.
- Pole a little lower and further forward when it’s windy.
- If it’s too windy to fly the kite safely — don’t.

## The death roll

The opposite: on a dead run the boat rolls to windward and crash-gybes. Prevent it by sailing a little higher (broad
reach), trimming the sheet on, and keeping the vang firm.
