Sailing is self-policing. If you break a rule, you’re expected to take a penalty — and if someone else does, you
can protest.

## Taking a penalty

<PenaltyTurns />

- **Two-Turns Penalty** — after breaking a rule of Part 2 (e.g. port/starboard, windward/leeward).
- **One-Turn Penalty** — after touching a mark (rule 31).
- Get **well clear** of other boats first, then do the turns **promptly**, in the same direction, each with a tack and a gybe.
- If you gained a significant advantage (or caused injury or serious damage), turns aren’t enough — you should **retire**.

The SIs can change the penalty (e.g. a scoring penalty instead of turns) — check them.

## Protesting

If you think another boat broke a rule involving you:

1. **Hail “Protest!”** at the first reasonable opportunity.
2. **Display a red flag** if your boat is longer than 6 m — keep it flying until you finish.
3. **Tell the race committee** after finishing if the SIs require it.
4. **Lodge a written protest** within the time limit in the SIs.

## The hearing

A protest committee listens to both boats and any witnesses, finds the facts, and decides which rule (if any) was
broken. It’s usually friendly and a great way to learn the rules. Bring a diagram!

<Callout type="tip">
  Most incidents in club racing are resolved on the water: the boat that broke the rule says “sorry, doing turns”,
  spins, and everyone carries on.
</Callout>
