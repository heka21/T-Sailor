Keelboats are very safe boats — but there are a few things on board that can hurt you if you don’t know about them.
Good crew make safety a habit, so they can relax and enjoy the sailing.

## Lifejackets

- Wear the lifejacket you are given, **fitted properly**: buckles done up, straps snug so it can’t ride up over your chin.
- Know which type it is. Many keelboat crews wear **inflatable** lifejackets — find the manual pull-toggle and check the green indicator before you go.
- The skipper decides when lifejackets must be worn. Many clubs require them whenever you are on deck while learning — and the rules may require them at times too.

## Moving around the boat

- **“One hand for you, one for the boat.”** Always hold on to something solid.
- Move along the **windward (high) side**, keeping low. It’s steadier and you won’t slide towards the water.
- Don’t stand on ropes or sails — they slide.
- Wear **non-slip, non-marking shoes**. Bare feet and toes don’t mix with winches and cleats.

## Sun, heat and cold

Perth summers are fierce on the water — the sun reflects off the river and the sea breeze makes it feel cooler than it is.

- Hat with a strap, polarised sunglasses on a cord, **SPF 50+** reapplied every couple of hours.
- Drink plenty of water.
- Bring a spray jacket even on warm days: wet + windy = cold, fast.

## Communication

Sailing is a team sport. The helm or skipper calls what is about to happen, and the crew answers so everyone knows
they’re ready.

| Call | Means | You answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Ready about?” | We’re about to tack | “Ready!” (only when you are) |
| “Lee-ho!” / “Tacking!” | The tack is happening now | — |
| “Ready to gybe?” | We’re about to gybe — boom will cross | “Ready!” |
| “Gybe-oh!” | Gybing now — heads down | — |
| “Man overboard!” | Someone is in the water | Point at them and keep pointing |

<Callout type="remember">
  If something feels wrong, say so. “Stop!” or “Wait!” from any crew member is always taken seriously.
</Callout>
