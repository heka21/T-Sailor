The skipper is responsible for the **boat, the crew and every decision** — from whether to go out at all, to who
does what in a tack. It doesn’t mean doing everything yourself. It means making sure everything gets done.

## The skipper’s responsibilities

- **Go / no-go** — checking the forecast, the boat and the crew’s experience before deciding to sail.
- **Safety** — lifejackets, the safety briefing, safety gear checked and known.
- **The plan** — where you’re going, how long, and what to do if conditions change.
- **Leadership** — clear, calm instructions. Ask for help, listen to the crew, and admit when to head home.

On a small keelboat the skipper is often also the helm. Give new crew one job at a time and rotate jobs so everybody learns.

## The safety briefing

Before leaving the dock, brief everyone — even experienced friends. Cover:

1. **Lifejackets** — how to wear them, when they must be worn.
2. **Man overboard** — shout, throw, point. Where the lifebuoy is.
3. **Safety gear** — flares, fire extinguisher, first-aid kit, bucket, radio/phone, anchor.
4. **Hazards** — the boom, winches, sheets, moving around the boat.
5. **The engine and the radio** — how to start and stop the engine and call for help if the skipper is incapacitated.
6. **The plan for today** and roles for leaving the mooring.

## Giving instructions

- Use the **standard calls** so everyone knows what’s coming.
- Say **who** and **what**: “Sam — ease the jib sheet a little.”
- **Wait for “Ready!”** before starting a manoeuvre.
- Explain **why** when there’s time — that’s how crews learn.
