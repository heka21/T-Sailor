Tacking and gybing are how a sailing boat changes direction across the wind. Both swap the sails from one side of
the boat to the other — but they’re very different.

- **Tack** — the **bow** turns through the wind. Sails flap briefly. Calm and controlled.
- **Gybe** — the **stern** turns through the wind. The sails stay full and the boom swings across with force.

### Crew job in a tack

1. On **“Ready about?”** — put 2 turns of the new (lazy) jib sheet on the new winch. Take the working sheet off its cleat/self-tailer but **hold** it. Answer **“Ready!”**
2. On **“Lee-ho!”** — keep holding the old sheet until the jib starts to back (flap towards you).
3. **Release** the old sheet cleanly — flick the turns off upward.
4. Move across, ducking under the boom.
5. **Pull the new sheet in fast**, hand over hand, while there’s no load. Then winch the final bit in and trim to the telltales.

<Callout type="tip">
  Timing is everything: release too early and the jib flaps uselessly; too late and the jib fills on the wrong side and
  pushes the bow back.
</Callout>

## Gybing

<TackGybe initial="gybe" />

### Crew job in a gybe

1. On **“Ready to gybe?”** — get the new jib sheet ready. Whoever has the mainsheet gets ready to haul it in.
2. As the helm bears away to a run, **pull the mainsheet in** so the boom is near the middle.
3. On **“Gybe-oh!”** — **heads down**. The boom comes across. Swap the jib sheets.
4. **Let the mainsheet out smoothly** on the new side — don’t let it crash out.

<Callout type="safety" title="The accidental gybe">
  On a run, if the boat turns too far or the wind shifts, the boom can come across without warning — a
  <strong>crash gybe</strong>. Stay low in the cockpit when sailing downwind and never sit in the boom’s path. Skippers
  often rig a <strong>preventer</strong> on long runs.
</Callout>
