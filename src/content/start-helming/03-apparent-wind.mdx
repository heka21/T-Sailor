Stand still on a calm day and you feel no wind. Ride a bike and you feel a breeze in your face — wind created by
your own movement. A sailing boat is the same: the sails feel the **true wind** plus the **wind made by the boat’s
speed**. The result is the **apparent wind**.

## What it means on the water

- **Apparent wind is always further forward than the true wind** (unless you’re going straight downwind).
- **Upwind, it’s stronger** than the true wind — the boat feels windier than it really is.
- **Downwind, it’s lighter** — which is why a run can feel calm, then turning upwind suddenly feels like a gale.
- **When the boat speeds up**, the apparent wind moves forward, so the sails need to be **sheeted in**.
- **In a gust**, the true wind strengthens before the boat speeds up, so the apparent wind briefly moves *aft*. Upwind, that lets you head up a little; downwind, sailors bear away with the gust.

<Callout type="tip" title="Telltales and burgees">
  Everything on the boat — the windex, telltales and sails — shows the apparent wind. A flag on a moored boat or the
  ripples on the water show the true wind.
</Callout>

## Why helms care

When you bear away from close-hauled to a reach, the boat accelerates, the apparent wind moves forward and the
sails can need *less* easing than you expect. Steering and trimming always go together.
