Keelboat sails generate a lot of force — far more than you can hold by hand in a breeze. Winches, cleats and
clutches let you control those loads safely.

## The winch

A winch is a drum that turns only one way (**clockwise**) and gives you mechanical advantage with a handle.

<Callout type="safety" title="Winch safety">
  <ul>
    <li>Rope always goes on <strong>clockwise</strong>.</li>
    <li>Keep hands at least a hand-width from the drum — never let fingers get dragged in.</li>
    <li>Take the handle out when you’re done and stow it in its pocket.</li>
    <li>If a rope crosses over itself on the drum (an <strong>override</strong>), it jams. Stop and call the skipper — don’t keep winding.</li>
  </ul>
</Callout>

## Cleats

- **Horn cleat** — the classic T-shape. Make fast with a cleat hitch (turn, figure-eights, locking hitch).
- **Cam cleat** — two spring-loaded jaws. Pull the rope down into them; pull up and out to release.
- **Clam cleat** — grooved, no moving parts. Rope jams in the V.

## Clutches (rope jammers)

Halyards and control lines often run through **clutches** near the cockpit. Lever **down** = locked, **up** =
released. Golden rule: **put the line on a winch and take the load before you open a clutch**, otherwise it can run away
fast.

## Coiling and stowing

A tidy cockpit is a safe cockpit. Coil the tails of sheets and halyards and stow them so they can run freely when
needed — never under your feet.
