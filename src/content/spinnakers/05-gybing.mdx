When you gybe with a spinnaker, the main and the pole both swap sides. The trick is to keep the kite flying in front
of the boat while the pole moves.

<Spinnaker mode="gybe" />

## End-for-end gybe (smaller keelboats)

1. Bear away to a **dead run**; square the pole back and keep the kite flying with sheet and guy.
2. Bow person **unclips the pole from the mast** and clips that end onto the old sheet (the new guy).
3. The main is gybed across.
4. Bow **releases the old guy** from the other end of the pole and clips it onto the mast. The pole has swapped ends.
5. Trim for the new course.

## Dip-pole gybe (bigger keelboats)

The pole stays attached to the mast. Its outboard end is **dipped** low enough to pass inside the forestay, the old
guy is released, and the new guy is clipped in on the other side before the pole is raised again. It needs a lazy
sheet and guy on each corner, and good timing between bow and pit.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steer slow, steer smooth">
  The helm’s job is to turn slowly through dead downwind and **match the turn to the crew**. If the kite is flying and
  in front of the boat, there’s no rush.
</Callout>

## If it goes wrong

- Kite collapsed and wrapping? Steer back to dead downwind behind the main to blanket it, sort it out, then re-fill.
- Pole stuck? Keep the boat on a run and fly the kite on sheet and guy alone until it’s clipped on.
