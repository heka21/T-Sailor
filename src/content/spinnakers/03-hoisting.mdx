Most hoists happen just after rounding the windward mark and bearing away. The goal: the spinnaker goes up fast,
fills cleanly and the boat accelerates — without the sail filling halfway up or wrapping round the forestay.

<Spinnaker mode="hoist" />

## Key points

- **Bear away first.** Hoisting on a broad reach or run puts the kite in the jib’s wind shadow so it goes up without filling too early.
- **Fast halyard.** The pit hauls hand-over-hand as fast as possible. A kite that fills halfway up is very hard to hoist.
- **Guy back, sheet on.** As it reaches the top, pull the guy to bring the tack to the pole, then trim the sheet to fill.
- **Jib away.** Once the spinnaker is drawing, drop or furl the jib so it doesn’t steal wind.

<Callout type="safety">
  Keep hands and feet away from the halyard and sheets while they run. A spinnaker in a breeze generates big loads —
  always have a turn on a winch before taking load.
</Callout>

## Common problems

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Twisted kite (hourglass) | Packed twisted, or hoisted slowly | Pull on the sheet and guy separately; worst case, drop and repack |
| Kite wraps around forestay | Pole or guy not back, jib down too early | Steer to blanket it behind the main, gybe if needed to unwrap |
| Kite fills halfway up | Halyard too slow, too much sheet | Ease the sheet, keep hauling |
