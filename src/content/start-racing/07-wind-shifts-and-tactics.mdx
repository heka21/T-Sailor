The wind is never perfectly steady — it swings left and right by 5–15° every few minutes. Sailing the shifts well is
the biggest upwind gain in racing.

## Lifts and headers

- **Lift** — the wind shifts so you can point **closer to the mark**. Good — keep going.
- **Header** — the wind shifts so you have to bear away, pointing **further from the mark**. When you’re headed,
  the *other* tack is lifted.

  If you get headed, tack — you’ll be on the lifted tack. Use your compass: note your heading on each tack, and you’ll
  know immediately whether you’re lifted or headed.
</Callout>

## Laylines

A **layline** is the line from which you can just sail to the mark close-hauled. Going beyond it (“overstanding”) is
wasted distance; getting there too early leaves you no options if the wind shifts. Stay inside the laylines until the
last part of the beat.

## Clear air

Boats to windward and ahead throw a **wind shadow** downwind of their sails. Avoid sailing in another boat’s dirty air —
tack away or bear off to find clean wind.

## Simple tactics

- **Go towards more pressure** — darker water means more wind.
- **Know the Doctor** — in a building sea breeze the wind often veers (shifts right) and strengthens; the right side may pay. Watch the leaders.
- **Cover** — when ahead, stay between the boats behind you and the next mark.
- **Consolidate** — when a shift has given you a gain, tack to put it in the bank.
- **Keep it simple** — boat speed and clear air win most club races.

## Downwind

It’s the mirror image: if a shift forces you to **head up away from the mark** to keep the spinnaker full, **gybe** —
the other gybe now points closer to the mark. And sail towards the pressure.
