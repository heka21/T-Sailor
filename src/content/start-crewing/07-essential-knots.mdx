You don’t need hundreds of knots — you need a handful you can tie **quickly, correctly and without thinking**,
even when the boat is moving. A good sailing knot is easy to tie, holds well and is easy to untie.

## Which knot when?

| Job | Knot |
| --- | --- |
| Stop a sheet running out of a block | Figure-eight |
| Tie the jib sheets to the sail / make a loop that won’t slip | Bowline |
| Hang a fender from a rail | Clove hitch (+ a half hitch) |
| Tie a line to a ring, post or rail under load | Round turn & two half hitches |
| Make a line fast on a horn cleat | Cleat hitch |
| Tie in a reef / tie sail ties | Reef knot |
| Join two ropes | Sheet bend |

<Callout type="tip" title="Practise off the boat">
  Keep a metre of rope next to the couch and tie each knot five times a day for a week. Then try them with your eyes
  closed — on a dark, wet boat that’s how you’ll need them.
</Callout>
