A sailing boat has no brakes. But there are reliable ways to slow down or stop — and every helm should know them.

## Heaving-to

Heaving-to “parks” the boat. It sits quietly at about 45–60° to the wind, slowly drifting sideways and a little forward.

<HeaveTo />

**How to heave-to:**

1. Sail close-hauled.
2. **Tack, but leave the jib sheet cleated** on the old side. The jib backs (fills from the wrong side).
3. Let the boat slow down, then **push the tiller to leeward** and lash or hold it there.
4. **Ease the mainsheet** until the boat sits steady without tacking or bearing away.

To sail off again: release the tiller, release the backed jib sheet and trim it on the correct side.

<Callout type="tip" title="Use it">
  Heaving-to is perfect for: putting in a reef, having lunch, waiting for a race start, dealing with a tangle, or
  checking on a seasick crew.
</Callout>

## Other ways to slow down

- **Luffing up** — head into the wind. The sails flap and the boat glides to a stop. Great for the final metres of picking up a mooring.
- **Easing the sails** on a close reach — let both sheets out until the sails flap. Trim back in to accelerate. This gives fine speed control.
- **Lying a-hull / drifting** — sails down. Only in open water with lots of room.
