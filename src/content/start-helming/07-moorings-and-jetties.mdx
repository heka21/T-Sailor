The hardest part of most sails is the first and last five minutes. Close to jetties and other boats, take your
time: **slow is pro**.

## Plan the approach

Before you go in, look at:

- **The wind** — which way will the boat drift when it stops?
- **Moored boats** — they point into the wind or current, showing you the best direction to approach from.
- **Your escape route** — if it goes wrong, where can you go round again?

## Coming alongside a jetty

- Have **fenders** out on the correct side and **lines** ready at the bow and stern — led outside everything and ready to step off with (not throw!).
- Approach at a shallow angle (~20–30°) and slowly. If possible approach **into the wind**, so it helps stop you.
- Crew step off only when the boat is close and nearly stopped. **Never jump** across a gap.
- Make the **spring** or **bow line** fast first to stop the boat, then the rest.

<Callout type="safety">
  Never use hands, feet or legs to stop a moving keelboat. Fenders and lines do the work. If you’re coming in too fast,
  abort and go round.
</Callout>

## Under engine

Many keelboats come into the pens under motor. Remember: the boat is still affected by wind, it steers poorly at very low
speed, and in reverse the stern often kicks to one side (**prop walk**). Small bursts of power, then neutral, keep you in control.
