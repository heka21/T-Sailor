**Reefing** reduces the area of the mainsail so the boat isn’t overpowered. A reefed boat heels less, steers more
easily and is often *faster* in a breeze — and much more comfortable for the crew.

## When to reef

- The boat is heeling so much the rail is in the water and the helm is fighting weather helm.
- You’re easing the main right out in every gust just to stay upright.
- The forecast says the breeze is coming — Fremantle Doctor afternoons, a front approaching.

<Callout type="remember" title="Old sailors’ rule">
  “The time to reef is when you first think about it.” It’s much easier to shake a reef out than to put one in when
  it’s already blowing hard.
</Callout>

## Reducing the headsail

On boats with a **furling jib**, roll it part-way in: ease the jib sheet a little and pull the furling line, then
re-trim. On older boats you may change down to a smaller jib.

## Other ways to depower first

Before reefing — or along with it — you can flatten the sails:

- **Backstay** tight — bends the mast and flattens the main.
- **Outhaul** and **cunningham** tight — flattens the main.
- **Traveller** down to leeward — spills wind from the main in gusts.
- **Jib cars** back — twists and flattens the top of the jib.
