As helm, you’re in charge of the manoeuvre: you look around, call it, steer it, and make sure the crew are ready.

## Tacking from the helm

<TackGybe />

1. **Look** — is there water and traffic clear on the new side and astern?
2. **Pick a landmark** about 90° to windward — that’s roughly where you’ll point after the tack.
3. **Call “Ready about?”** and wait for **“Ready!”** from the crew.
4. **Call “Lee-ho!”** and push the tiller smoothly towards the sail (to leeward). Not a shove — a firm, steady push.
5. **Cross the boat** facing forward as the boom comes over, swapping hands on the tiller extension behind your back or in front.
6. **Straighten up** on the new close-hauled course — don’t over-turn into a reach.

<Callout type="tip" title="Speed first">
  Start the tack with good speed. A boat that’s pinching or slow can stall head-to-wind and get stuck in irons. If that
  happens: have the crew hold the jib out to one side (back it) so the wind pushes the bow round, then sheet in on the
  correct side once the sails fill and the boat starts moving.
</Callout>

## Gybing from the helm

<TackGybe initial="gybe" />

1. **Look all round**, especially downwind on the new side.
2. **Call “Ready to gybe?”** and wait for **“Ready!”**
3. Bear away to a run while the mainsheet is trimmed in.
4. **Call “Gybe-oh!”** as you turn the stern through the wind — **gently**. A small turn is enough.
5. As the boom crosses, **steady up straight away** — the boat will try to round up on the new side. Meet it with opposite helm.

## What can go wrong

| Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Stuck in irons after a tack | Back the jib to push the bow round, then sheet in once moving |
| Boat spins up after a gybe | Less turn, more counter-steer, ease main as it crosses |
| Crew not ready | Never start until you’ve heard “Ready!” |
