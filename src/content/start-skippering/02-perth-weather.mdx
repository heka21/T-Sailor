Perth has some of the most reliable sailing breezes in the world — and a few traps. A good skipper knows the local
pattern and checks the forecast every time.

## The summer pattern

On a typical summer day:

1. **Morning — easterly.** A warm, gusty, often offshore breeze blowing from the land. Shifty on the river near the shore.
2. **Late morning — calm patch.** The easterly fades as the land heats up.
3. **Afternoon — the Fremantle Doctor.** A cool **south-westerly sea breeze** pushes in from the ocean, reaching Fremantle first, then up the Swan to Matilda Bay. It often builds to **15–25 knots**.
4. **Evening** — the Doctor usually eases around sunset.

## Winter

From May to September, **cold fronts** cross the south-west:

- Before a front: winds back to the **north / north-west** and strengthen, often with cloud.
- As the front passes: a sudden shift to the **west / south-west**, squalls, rain and gusts.
- After: cooler south-westerlies and showers.

Fronts can bring gale-force gusts. If one is forecast, plan to be back early — or don’t go.

## Reading a forecast

Use the **Bureau of Meteorology (BOM)** coastal waters forecast and observations, plus the app’s [Weather](/weather) page.

- **Wind speed** is an average over 10 minutes — **gusts** can be 40% stronger.
- **Strong wind warnings** (from 26 knots) mean training is very unlikely.
- Look at the **trend** across the day, not just the current number.

## Go / no-go

Ask yourself:

- Is the forecast within this crew’s and boat’s limits — including the gusts and the afternoon breeze?
- Is a change (front, sea breeze, storm) expected while we’re out?
- Do we have a plan B (reef, shorter sail, head home early)?

If the answer to any is “not sure”, the answer is usually **don’t go yet**.
