Wind is your engine. Before you can trim a sail or steer a course, you have to know **where the wind is coming
from** — and that changes all the time.

## Wind is named for where it comes FROM

A **southerly** blows *from* the south. The **Fremantle Doctor** is a **south-westerly**: it comes *from* the south-west
and blows towards the north-east. Forecasts and sailors always describe wind this way.

## Ways to find the wind

- **Feel it on your face and ears.** Turn your head until the wind sounds the same in both ears — you’re facing it.
- **The masthead indicator (windex).** It points *into* the wind (like an arrow showing where wind comes from).
- **Telltales and flags.** Ribbons on the shrouds or a flag on a moored boat stream away from the wind.
- **The water.** Ripples and dark patches (“cats’ paws”) show gusts coming. Waves move with the wind.
- **Moored boats.** Boats on moorings swing to point into the wind (or into the current if it’s stronger).

<Callout type="tip" title="Look upwind">
  Glance upwind regularly. Dark, ruffled water heading your way is a gust — warn the crew: “Puff coming!”
</Callout>

## Windward and leeward

Once you know where the wind is, you can describe the boat:

- **Windward** — the side the wind hits first. When the boat heels, it’s the high side. The crew sits here.
- **Leeward** (“loo-ard”) — the side away from the wind. The sails and boom are on this side.
